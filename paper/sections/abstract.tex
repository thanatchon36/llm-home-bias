\begin{abstract}
\noindent Do open-weight language models allocate more of an equity portfolio to the stock market of the country where they were built? We ask \nModelsRun{} models from China, the United States, Europe, Thailand and Korea, run locally, to split a portfolio across eight markets in 240 factorial prompts that vary the investor's residence, the prompt language, risk tolerance and horizon. In the pre-registered regression a model's home market receives \betaHome{}\,pp (95\% CI \betaHomeLo{} to \betaHomeHi{}) relative to what other models give that market: no home tilt, and if anything a small negative one. A post-hoc check that allows for each model's dispersion around equal weight puts the effect at \betaPosthocDisp{}\,pp. The investor's stated residence is associated with a far larger shift of \gammaInv{}\,pp towards that market, and the prompt language with \lambdaLang{}\,pp. Thai fine-tuning moves the weight on Thailand by \ftTyphoontwoEightb{}\,pp in one pair of models and by \ftOpenthaigptoneFiveSevenb{}\,pp in the other. All prompts, raw replies and code are public.
\end{abstract}
